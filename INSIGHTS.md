# What the toll says, and where the summer money should go

Figures use BITRE deaths current to 30 September 2026 and the ABS population at 31 March 2026. The path halves each state's 2018–2020 average by the end of 2030 in a straight line. The national baseline is 1139.3 deaths a year.

## The decision

Holiday-season enforcement and infrastructure money should go to four places at once:

1. **Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and Western Australia**, in that order of lives above the path (138, 122, 105, 88). Together they are most of the national gap of 522.
2. **Regional roads that sit above their own state**, led by Western Australia's Wheat Belt, Queensland's Wide Bay and Darling Downs, Victoria's Hume corridor, and New South Wales' New England, Central West and Capital region. The Northern Territory is the highest risk per person at 13.0 deaths per 100,000, against 4.8 nationally.
3. **People outside cars, without dropping drivers.** Drivers are still the largest gap (201 above the path, 586 deaths). Motorcyclists (124 above the path), pedestrians (85) and cyclists (26) have the sharpest rises. Cyclist deaths are about double the path.
4. **Afternoons and faster roads.** The worst hour in the last 12 months is 4pm (93 deaths). In Victoria, about 9 in 100 fatal-or-serious crashes on 90 km/h and faster roads were fatal, against about 2 in 100 on streets up to 50 km/h. Most Victorian fatal crashes were not at an intersection.

South Australia does not have the largest gap, but it has already failed 2026: 84 deaths from January to September, against a full-year ceiling of 67. Western Australia's latest 12-month toll is 201, its worst September-ending year since 2010.

## The national miss

The 12 months to September 2026: **1,334 deaths**. The path allowed **812** (unrounded 811.8). **522 deaths above the path.** Rate: **4.8 per 100,000**.

Full calendar years rose every year from 2021 to 2025 (1,129, 1,182, 1,252, 1,291, 1,306). January–September 2026 was 1,001, above 973 in the same months of 2025. That is the sixth year of the rise, and the highest January–September total since 2009. It is already above the full-year ceiling for 2026 (798).

Children aged 0–7: **31 deaths** in the last 12 months. The 2030 aim is zero. Age was missing for 15 of the latest deaths, so age rates exclude them.

Men died at 7.1 per 100,000 and women at 2.5. Men are about three quarters of the toll.

People 75 and over (7.3 per 100,000) and people 17–25 (7.1) have the highest age-specific rates. People 40–64 contribute the most extra deaths because the group is large.

## Summer is not a different crash

Over 2022–2025, December and January averaged 3.42 deaths a day and the other months averaged 3.45. Summer is not a spike in daily risk. It is when agencies already put people on the road, and last summer (December 2025 and January 2026) was still 216 deaths against a path of 143.

Use that operation on the year-round gaps above. Do not design it as if only holiday weekends kill.

## Communities, and how sure we are

Deaths in a single community bounce around. A model shares strength across communities in the same state and across road-user groups, using deaths and population for 2022–2025. A place is listed only when at least five people died in that group over those four years and the likely range for its rate sits entirely above the state rate.

The clearest extra deaths per year are regional driver tolls: Western Australia - Wheat Belt (WA, driver, about 15 extra deaths a year); Wide Bay (QLD, driver, about 12 extra deaths a year); Hume (VIC, driver, about 9 extra deaths a year); New England and North West (NSW, driver, about 9 extra deaths a year); Capital Region (NSW, driver, about 8 extra deaths a year); Central West (NSW, driver, about 7 extra deaths a year); Darling Downs - Maranoa (QLD, driver, about 7 extra deaths a year); Shepparton (VIC, driver, about 7 extra deaths a year).

The check on the model: it expected about the same total deaths as actually occurred across those communities and groups, and the share of community-years with no deaths was close. Details are in `data/model/ppc.json` after `make model`.

## What this does not show

There is no measure here of kilometres driven, registered motorcycles, or hours of enforcement, so a high rate is not proof of why the risk is high. Recent months will be revised. Victoria's crash file is a different collection and stops being complete in 2025; it is used for road environment, not for the national death total. About 100 of the latest deaths have no remoteness area recorded. The state paths apply the national halving rule to each state. The Strategy itself sets a national target.
